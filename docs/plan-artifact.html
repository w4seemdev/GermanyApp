01What we're building

A calm, fast brochure site for a Dortmund agency that helps mostly Arabic-speaking residents through German bureaucracy — naturalisation, documents, marriage and translation, study and visas, finance, real estate — plus a commercial cleaning arm.

It is a showcase site with exactly one moving part: a contact form that emails the owner. We build the complete UI; a separate backend developer implements the send.

Two things make it harder than it looks, and neither is optional:

  • It is genuinely bilingual with full RTL — and the client's German and Arabic source documents are not translations of each other. They differ in structure, not just wording.
  • The copy is legally load-bearing. In Germany, legal, tax and insurance advice are licensed activities. The client's own copy is carefully hedged, and their live draft already carries the line "Keine Rechtsberatung." Rewriting it for punchiness creates real liability.

04Design concept — "Split"

The duality is real, so make it the system.

The business has two arms and says so itself on its live draft: "Wie können wir Ihnen helfen? Zwei Leistungsbereiche, ein verlässlicher Ansprechpartner." The logo encodes the same duality — a document and a broom. And there are two languages. The developer's own "split app concept" instinct has business justification behind it, not just visual appeal.

Build it as an asymmetric split system, not a 50/50 gimmick: one SplitSection primitive with ratio tokens (1.03:0.97 at the hero → 0.8:1.2 at contact), varied deliberately down the page. Three reasons it's architecturally right and not merely decorative:

  1. CSS Grid column order follows dir — the whole motif mirrors into Arabic with zero extra code. The visual concept and the RTL requirement are aligned rather than fighting.
  2. Six bespoke section layouts collapse into six compositions of one primitive. At $700 that is real time.
  3. The reference site has splits; it has no system of them. That's the difference the client is paying for.
Grafted from the runner-up concept

The client's own closing line in both PDFs is "You have a matter and don't know where to begin?" — the product is removing that uncertainty. So the home page gains a 3-step process strip: Anliegen schildern → Gemeinsam sortieren → Nächste Schritte. It costs one row, it's the best anxiety-reducer available, and it is the legally safest way to describe the business — a process description makes no outcome promise.

Arabic is not "German in a different font"

Four things change, and this is where most bilingual sites fail:

  • Display sizes go down ~12–17% — Arabic ascenders and descenders make headlines run taller and heavier; a Latin-sized Arabic h1 looks shouty and wraps to two lines.
  • Body sizes go up ~5–8% — Arabic glyphs are optically smaller relative to the em.
  • Line-height increases substantially — body 1.90 vs 1.65, h1 1.28 vs 0.96.
  • Letter-spacing must be exactly 0 and text-transform must never fire. Tracking breaks the cursive joins between Arabic letterforms. The eyebrow's 0.16em tracking and uppercase are disabled in Arabic entirely.
The bidi bug that will otherwise ship

Every Latin run inside Arabic copy — phone numbers, emails, +49, "Zukunft Service" — must be wrapped in <bdi dir="ltr">. Without it +49 177 3825632 renders as 3825632 177 49+ and the user cannot dial the number. It's the most common Arabic-web bug and it will hit the info strip, which now carries a confirmed phone number.

مثال: اتصل بنا على +49 177 3825632 من الاثنين إلى الجمعة.

Typeface: IBM Plex Serif + Sans + Sans Arabic — one superfamily whose Arabic was drawn to the same brief as the Latin with matched vertical metrics, so both locales read as one brand rather than a bolted-on script. Loaded via next/font, which self-hosts — removing the Google Fonts exposure that produced roughly 100,000 Abmahnungen after LG München I, 3 O 17493/20.

06Architecture

Next.js 16 App Router + React 19 + TypeScript 5.9 + Tailwind v4, every route prerendered. Astro is the honest runner-up — less JS, arguably the better brochure tool — but the brief hard-requires React as the handed-over artifact, and the React form island drags React back in anyway. A plain Vite SPA is rejected outright: no link previews, no server-rendered hreflang, and a visible LTR→RTL flash on every Arabic page load.

Nine runtime dependencies, ~25 KB gzipped: next, react, react-dom, react-hook-form, zod, @hookform/resolvers, clsx, lucide-react.

Explicitly rejected: framer-motion (~34 KB for what @keyframes does here) · any i18n library (they assume key symmetry; ours is asymmetric by design) · Radix/shadcn (native <dialog> gives focus trap, Esc, inert and backdrop for 0 KB) · any state library (not one piece of state is read by two unrelated subtrees) · carousel libraries (CSS scroll-snap is RTL-correct with no configuration) · reCAPTCHA (US processor, consent trigger).

RTL in three layers

  1. ~95% — CSS logical properties via Tailwind v4. pl- pr- ml- mr- left- right- text-left text-right are banned, enforced by a ~25-line build check. A code-review convention won't survive a deadline; a failing build will.
  2. ~4% — rtl:/ltr: variants where no logical property exists.
  3. ~1% — custom @utility with a [dir="rtl"] override for gradients, the one thing CSS has no logical syntax for.

Shadow mirroring is eliminated, not solved — every shadow token has an x-offset of 0. Grid and flex mirror for free, which is the real argument for building the split motif on Grid. Arrows and chevrons mirror by swapping the component, not CSS-scaling; the logo, checkmarks, phone, clock, map-pin, the WhatsApp glyph and any numeral never mirror.

Content model — the highest-risk decision

The Arabic PDF omits the entire post-arrival support block, merges three finance sub-blocks into one flat list, has richer "Warum" copy than the German, and lists cleaning types differently. Every service is modelled as ServiceBlock[] — a flat list is one untitled block, sub-blocks are several titled ones — so the two locales differ in block count without differing in type.

export type ServiceContentMap = Record<Locale, Record<ServiceId, ServiceContent>>;
//                                     ↑ spine: both locales, all six, mandatory
//                                                            ↑ body inside is free

The spine (service ids, why-points, nav, all UI microcopy, every title and meta description) is type-enforced in both locales — TypeScript errors on any omission. The body may diverge freely, with a CONTENT_GAPS registry turning every divergence into a line the client initials, and unregistered drift into a build failure.

Hard rule

Never machine-translate hedged legal copy. Any Arabic we author carries status: 'draft-needs-client-approval' and does not go live without written client sign-off. Getting a hedging verb wrong in regulated subject matter means the client carries the liability.

07The contact form

The entire functional surface of the site. Everything else can be judged by eye; this is the only thing that can be broken.

Two invariants: submitContactForm() never throws — every failure is a value in a discriminated union, so there's no try/catch anywhere in a component. And the backend developer changes zero frontend files in the happy path: he sets one env var and implements a documented contract. If his API shape differs, he edits exactly one ~40-line function.

Field order — deliberately not the conventional one

Do not open with "Name." Asking a user worried about their residency status to identify themselves first is the highest-friction possible opening.

  1. "Worum geht es?" — service select, six options plus "Sonstiges / Ich bin mir nicht sicher." That seventh option is the most important control on the form: the client's own headline is "you don't know where to start?", so a required select with no escape hatch would contradict the brand promise.
  2. The message — second, so the user tells their story before being asked who they are.
  3. Name, then email (required, it's the Reply-To), phone (optional; required only if WhatsApp opt-in is ticked).
  4. Preferred contact time — and because the confirmed hours are 10:00–16:00, the options are Vormittags (10–13) / Nachmittags (13–16) / Egal. Never offer an evening slot the business cannot honour.

Phone validation is deliberately permissive — no libphonenumber (78–145 KB for one optional field), and the hint reads "z. B. +49 oder +963." A form implying German numbers only reads as exclusionary to exactly the audience this business serves; a false rejection is a lost lead, and the receiving human is the real validator.

The contract

export interface ContactFormPayload {
  name: string;                  // 2–80, no CR/LF
  email: string;                 // Reply-To — NEVER From
  phone: PhoneValue | null;      // { raw, e164, assumedCountry }
  whatsappOptIn: boolean;
  serviceCategory: ServiceCategory;
  message: string;               // 10–2000, untrusted — escape before HTML email
  preferredContactTime: ContactTime;
  privacyNoticeVersion: string;
  locale: 'de' | 'ar';           // REPLY IN THIS LANGUAGE
  meta: { submittedAt, elapsedMs, linkCount, pagePath, timezone, clientVersion };
}

export type ContactFormResult =
  | { ok: true; referenceId: string | null }
  | { ok: false; error: ContactFormError };

The mock ships enabled by default, with magic addresses (429@test.de, 500@test.de, offline@test.de, slow@test.de) that demo every UI state with no backend at all — worth real money on a fixed fee, because it lets you get sign-off and close the UI phase without waiting on anyone.

Three things that silently destroy the feature
  • From: must be the site's own domain, never the submitter's address. Setting From: user@gmail.com fails Gmail's DMARC and the owner's notification is rejected or spam-filed. The most common contact-form mistake in existence.
  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC on zukunftservice.de. Without them notifications quietly disappear and the client concludes "the website doesn't work."
  • Strip \r/\n from anything interpolated into a mail header. SMTP header injection. We block it at the edge too, but the edge is bypassable.

Spam: honeypot plus a 3-second timing trap, and nothing else in v1. Both trigger silent success — the bot logs a win and gets no signal to tune against. Turnstile and hCaptcha are deferred deliberately: they're US processors loaded on page render, which for a German site means a privacy-declaration entry, an AV-Vertrag, and an argument about consent-gating your own contact form.

08Compliance, accessibility, SEO

The commercial risk

A missing or defective Impressum is actionable under §3a UWG. Typical Streitwert €1,500–€5,000, first-letter fees €300–€900, contractual penalties €2,500–€5,100 per repeat. Shipping without a complete Impressum can cost more than the entire project fee on the first letter.

The obligation is now §5 DDG, not §5 TMG — the TMG was replaced on 14 May 2024. Boilerplate still citing "§5 TMG" quotes a repealed provision and signals to an Abmahn-lawyer that nobody reviewed the site. There is no small-business exemption. The link must be labelled literally Impressum (case law has rejected "Kontakt", "Legal", "Info"). Also: the EU ODR platform shut down on 20 July 2025 — any boilerplate still linking it must be deleted.

The mitigation is mechanical: every still-missing Impressum field is typed with a «…» sentinel that renders loudly in dev and fails the production build. ~15 lines, and it makes shipping a placeholder Impressum impossible.

Four regulated activities need a direct question to the client, because each — if licensed — adds mandatory Impressum fields, and if unlicensed makes the hedged copy load-bearing: §34d GewO (insurance mediation — "Lebensversicherung, Alters- und Zukunftsvorsorge" is squarely in this territory and is the highest-risk copy on the site), §34c/§34i GewO (real estate and loans, including the Dubai section), RDG, and Handwerkskammer registration for the cleaning arm.

Enforce the hedging in the build, not in a document

A copy constraint written in Word is violated within one edit cycle. Ship a banned-lexicon lint over src/content/** in CI — /\bwir beraten\b/i, /\bgarantier/i, /نضمن/, /استشارة\s*قانونية/ — plus a <HedgeNotice> mandatory on the Finanzen, Immobilien and Behörden pages. This converts a legal-exposure requirement into a build gate: ~90 minutes, and the cheapest risk mitigation in the plan.

No cookie banner — and the rules that hold it

Zero cookies, zero third-party requests ⇒ §25 TDDDG never triggers ⇒ no banner. This is better at any budget: a banner costs design time, a11y work (it's a focus-trap modal on first paint), CLS budget and conversion — it is a wall between an anxious first-time visitor and the page.

Held by hard rules: no Google Fonts CDN · no Maps iframe (address card + an "In Google Maps öffnen ↗" link instead) · no video embeds · no analytics in v1 · no CDN-hosted assets. Enforced by a build script that greps the output for external origins and fails, plus Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'self'. One localStorage key is approved — the chosen locale, strictly necessary under §25 Abs. 2 Nr. 2.

Two ARIA details routinely got wrong

  • Individual field errors are not live regions — that produces a machine-gun of announcements while tabbing. They're announced via aria-describedby on focus, with the error id first so the problem is heard before the generic hint.
  • The error summary uses focus movement alone, not role="alert". Adding both causes a double announcement.

Performance

Test device: a 3–5-year-old mid-range Android on a German mobile network, often on prepaid data. Targets — LCP ≤ 2.0s · INP ≤ 150ms · CLS ≤ 0.03 · initial JS ≤ 80 KB gz · fonts ≤ 110 KB per locale · third-party requests: 0 — enforced by size-limit and Lighthouse CI as build-failing gates. A budget not enforced by a failing build is a wish.

Free win available immediately

The reference site ships ~6.4 MB of unoptimised PNG, including an 883 KB logo rendered at 168×68 px. Correct sizing plus AVIF/WebP drops the image payload by well over 90%.

One expectation to set in writing now: verticals 1–5 are long-tail and trust-driven. The real discovery engine for this business is Google Business Profile, reviews and WhatsApp sharing — not organic search. Only cleaning has genuine transactional volume. GBP setup is a separate, priced deliverable.

09Conversion notes

The visitor is on a phone, in the evening, holding a German letter they don't fully understand. They've already been turned away from one Amt for a missing document, aren't sure whether contacting you costs money, and are quietly worried that contacting a private company could affect their case.

Standard SaaS conversion patterns actively backfire here.

  • A real face and a real name is the highest-impact single element on the site. This audience buys a person, not a company. One portrait with a first-person line beats four abstract benefit cards. Will the client publish a portrait? If not, this lever is gone and the design is materially weaker.
  • "Wir sprechen Arabisch" must be visible on the German page, in Arabic script, in the hero trust row — not hidden behind the language toggle. Much of this audience lands on /de/ from Google and needs to know in one second.
  • WhatsApp is co-equal with email, not a fallback. The confirmed number is a mobile line, which strongly suggests it's already the owner's working channel. Pre-fill the message with the service name so the user never faces a blank box — it also arrives pre-qualified.
  • Show the hours as reassurance, not data. "Mo–Fr 10–16 Uhr" plus "Außerhalb der Öffnungszeiten: schreiben Sie uns — wir melden uns am nächsten Werktag" converts a closed-office moment into a captured lead instead of a bounce.
  • No stock photos of smiling business people. This audience has seen a thousand scam pages and recognises stock instantly — worse than no photo. Three real phone photos of the actual office beat fifteen stock images.
  • No countdown timers, no "only 3 slots left." Predatory-adjacent, poisons a trust-driven sale, and false scarcity is UWG-actionable in Germany anyway.
  • Specificity beats claims. "Wir organisieren syrische und irakische Personenstandsdokumente" proves domain knowledge; "Erfahren und zuverlässig" is what every scam site says. The itemised lists are the trust signal — surface them, don't summarise them into benefit-speak.

12Open questions

Address, phone, email and hours are Confirmed. What remains:

Blocking the build

  1. Exact legal name and legal form — Einzelunternehmen, GbR, UG or GmbH — plus Handelsregister court and number if registered, and USt-IdNr. or confirmation of Kleinunternehmer status under §19 UStG.
  2. Do they hold §34d GewO (insurance), §34c/§34i GewO (real estate/loans), or Handwerkskammer registration? Each adds mandatory Impressum fields and changes how far the copy may go.
  3. Who supplies the Datenschutzerklärung text, and by when?
  4. Who reviews and signs off the Arabic before launch? Scope is full bilingual, so this sits on the critical path. Any Arabic we author to fill gaps in the client's PDFs describes regulated activities and needs a named human to approve the wording.

Blocking the design

  1. Real photos or none? Only four images exist, all AI-generated stock — and the people in them read as European while the audience is Arabic-speaking Syrians and Iraqis, which undercuts the "mehrsprachig" trust promise. Ninety minutes with a modern phone beats any stock library here.
  2. Will the client publish a portrait and their name?
  3. Logo source files — SVG or ≥1000px transparent PNG. Is there an Arabic lockup variant?
  4. Which slogan? Three are in circulation: Viele Anliegen. Ein Ansprechpartner. (PDF), Viele Lösungen. Ein Ansprechpartner. (PDF footer), Viele Leistungen. Eine Anlaufstelle. (live site).
  5. Two golds acceptable? We ship #c48a16 for fills and #8a6013 for text. The client asked us to match the colours and will see two. Show a comparison before build.
  6. Arabic numerals — Western 0–9 (recommended: the audience transacts with German forms and phone numbers) or Eastern ٠–٩?
  7. Arabic grammatical gender — masculine-singular default or dual-form? A cultural call for the client.

Blocking launch

  1. What response time will the client commit to? It goes on the page. A promised 24 h that becomes five days is worse than promising 2–3 working days and hitting it.
  2. Was the Arabic omission of the post-arrival support block deliberate or an oversight? Everything about the audience says oversight — it's the strongest differentiator in the whole deck, and Arabic-speaking students are exactly who needs it.
  3. Hosting and domain — who owns the accounts, is hosting in the EU, is an AVV in place, does the fee include deployment?
  4. Any analytics, ever? If yes, the no-banner position ends and the privacy declaration must be rewritten.
  5. Tell the client their current site shows outdated opening hours. Small, but it's wrong information in front of customers today.

A 12-agent planning workflow: five research specialists completed — content and IA, design system, frontend architecture, the contact-form contract, and German compliance/SEO/accessibility. The two design-concept agents, the synthesizer, three adversarial critics and the finalizer were lost to a session limit.

Consequences worth knowing: the design concept in §4 is a convergence of the research, not a competed-and-judged winner.
